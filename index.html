Ear Pitch — the tone inside your ear

A pure high pitch, played steady or pulsing. High pitches like this can't be placed by your ears the way normal sounds can, so they feel like they're ringing inside your ear instead of coming out of the phone — even though the phone's speaker is what's making them. With earbuds, the binaural mode plays a slightly different pitch in each ear, and the beat between them is heard inside your head — that one genuinely isn't coming from the phone speaker at all.

Earbuds only. Your left ear gets the pitch, your right ear gets it a few Hz higher, and your brain hears the beat in the middle of your head.

How it works

The ear pitch: a pure high tone (14–19.5 kHz), steady or pulsing. Your ears locate normal sounds by the tiny time and loudness differences between your two ears — at these pitches those differences nearly vanish, so the tone feels like it's ringing inside your ear rather than sitting in the phone. That's the strange in-your-ear feeling.

The binaural beat (earbuds): each ear gets its own pitch, a few Hz apart. No beat exists in the air — your brain builds it, and you hear it in the middle of your head. On the phone speaker this doesn't work, because both ears hear both tones mixed together.

The whisper: your phone's own voice reads your words quietly, a little higher and slower than normal, with a soft breath sound mixed underneath — like someone whispering right next to you.

Honest limit: a phone speaker can't make a true silent ultrasound beam — that needs special hardware. This is the closest a phone gets: a pure high pitch that feels like it's in your ear, and a beat your brain makes itself.
